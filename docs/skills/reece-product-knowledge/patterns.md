# Patterns — Reece Product Knowledge

## Warranty Coverage Check
**When to use**: any "is this covered?" question.
**How**: 1) Identify the part (vinyl / IG / service / glass breakage). 2) Check exclusions: act of God, pre-existing or room-side condensation, windows removed, unpaid balance. 3) Check eligibility: original owner vs successor; transfer filed within 15 days of settlement. 4) State remedy: repair/replace, free parts/material/glass, and **free labor under the Free Service Pledge**. 5) Claim = certified mail + install date + certificate number/date + proof of purchase. (Ch 5)
**Trade-offs**: acts of God stay excluded, so storm damage isn't a warranty claim.

## Condensation Triage
**When to use**: "my windows are sweating/foggy."
**How**: Ask where the moisture is. Room-side → humidity education + fixes. Exterior → normal temperature differential. Between panes → seal failure, IG warranty claim. Walls/ceilings/mold → HVAC contractor. (Ch 7)
**Trade-offs**: telling a new customer "it's your humidity" can sound defensive — lead with the mirror analogy and the drafty-old-windows explanation.

## Energy Comparison ("Look for the Label")
**When to use**: comparing Conservation to another quote.
**How**: Compare NFRC whole-window labels (U-Factor, SHGC, VT, AL, CR) product to product. Use center-of-glass tables and the headline claims (equal to 5 panes, 99% UV, 50% quieter) to explain *why* the glass performs. Florida cooling → lead with SHGC; comfort → room-side temperature table. (Ch 2)
**Trade-offs**: Conservation Glass gives up some daylight (VT 66% vs 81%) for heat rejection.

## Like-for-Like Spec Comparison
**When to use**: customer compares a competitor's window to Conservation.
**How**: Walk the 33-point chart feature by feature, and compare energy on NFRC whole-window labels. Do not offer a refund or price-match: the 30-Day Price Guarantee is unconfirmed — don't use. (Ch 4, Ch 5)
**Trade-offs**: several items are proprietary/patented (Ship-Loc, Conservation Glass®, Entry-Guard), so true like-for-like matches are rare — say so respectfully, not dismissively.

## Style Selection
**When to use**: recommending a window type.
**How**: Operation constraint first (walkway/deck → slider; sink → garden), then architecture (traditional → double-hung or single-hung; modern → casement/awning; Victorian → bay/bow), then color/grids/glass. (Ch 1, Ch 8 for single-hung)
**Trade-offs**: casements open outward — check clearance just like projecting windows. Single-hung is offered but unsourced in the four PDFs, so confirm availability and stop there — no feature or approval claims for it.

## Safe Care Routine
**When to use**: post-install handoff, maintenance questions.
**How**: Rinse → mild soap/water → soft cloth → rinse → dry, one window at a time, in shade. Monthly on the coast, yearly minimum inland. Oil hinges with mineral/plant oil; never oil slider wheels; keep tracks and weeps clear. (Ch 6)
**Trade-offs**: none; improper tools risk voiding the warranty.

## Hurricane Glass Positioning
**When to use**: storm, security, or noise conversations.
**How**: Weakest-point argument → "breaks but stays bonded" → pick the benefit (debris / burglary / noise / energy) the homeowner cares about → cite test (9 lb 2×4 at 50 ft/s) and HVHZ/Miami-Dade approval. (Ch 3)
**Trade-offs**: never "proof" or guaranteed; hurricanes are excluded from the service pledge and glass breakage warranty.

## Wording Filter for Copy
**When to use**: before product facts from this skill go into customer-facing copy.
**How**: Manufacturer stats go in as written (they're approved). Swap out only the retired wording: in-house crew line → "Factory-Trained, Reece-Certified Crews"; "Reece Builders" → Reece Windows & Doors; never "hurricane-proof." Warranty headline = "transferable double lifetime warranty, parts and labor." (Ch 8)
**Trade-offs**: none; stats stay, only phrasing changes.
