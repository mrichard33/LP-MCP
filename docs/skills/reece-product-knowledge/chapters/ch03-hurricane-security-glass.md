# Chapter 3: Hurricane Glass, Security & Disaster Protection

**Source:** *Conservation Windows* brochure — "Offering Security & Hurricane Protection" page

## Core Idea
Windows are a building's weakest point because glass guards most of the opening. Conservation's Hurricane laminated glass keeps fragments bonded when broken, which delivers four benefits from one product: storm/debris protection, burglary resistance, noise reduction, and (with Low-E3) energy/UV control.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Weakest-point argument**: the opening is only as strong as its glass → upgrade the glass, not just the frame.
  - When to use: explaining why impact glass matters for Florida homes.
- **Four-benefit stack of laminated glass**: Disaster Protection · Security · Soundproofing/noise · Energy (Low-E3).
  - How: lead with the benefit the homeowner cares about; the others ride along.

## Key Concepts
- **Laminated glass**: glass layers bonded to a plastic interlayer; stays in the frame when broken.
- **Impact glass / Hurricane glass**: laminated glass tested against wind-borne debris (large-missile 2×4 test).
- **HVHZ**: High Velocity Hurricane Zone — Miami-Dade and Broward's stricter code region.
- **Low-E3**: triple-silver low-E coating applied to laminated impact glass.
- **Standard home-invasion devices**: ordinary tools a burglar would use to smash through.

## Mental Models
- Think of laminated glass as **"breaks but doesn't open."** Every benefit comes from that.
- Use the security angle for homeowners who don't feel storm risk; use the debris angle after storm season.

## Anti-patterns
- **Guaranteeing storm performance** ("hurricane-proof," "storm-proof," "will survive a Cat 5"): the brochure says "tested to withstand" and "effectively protect," not guarantees. Reece canon bans storm-performance guarantees (see reece-story-canon).
- **Claiming unbreakable glass**: the source explicitly says it *may* break.
- **Implying the whole line is impact-rated**: the brochure describes "a Hurricane glass impact line" — a specific line, not every Conservation window.
- **Citing specific code sections or approval numbers**: none are in these sources.

## Key Takeaways
1. Hurricane glass = two panes + interlayer 3× thicker than a windshield's.
2. Tested: multiple hits from a 9 lb 2×4 at 50 ft/s; approved for HVHZ incl. Miami-Dade.
3. Breaks but stays bonded → protects from debris, intruders and flying shards.
4. Bonus: noise reduction and Low-E3 energy/UV control.
5. Describe performance as "tested" and "approved," never "proof" or guaranteed.
