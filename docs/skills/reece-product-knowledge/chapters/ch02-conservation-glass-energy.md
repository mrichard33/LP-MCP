# Chapter 2: Conservation Glass & Energy Performance

**Source:** *Conservation Windows* brochure (energy, NFRC, ENERGY STAR, glass composition, Cardinal Endur IG pages)

## Core Idea
Conservation Glass is a dual-glazed, argon-filled insulated unit with a triple-silver low-E coating that rejects solar heat without tinting the view. Every energy claim in the line traces back to this glass and to NFRC/ENERGY STAR ratings.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **"Beats the Heat… Bold on Cold… Performs above the Norm"** — the brochure's three-part energy argument:
  - *Beats the Heat* (cooling): spectrally selective coating rejects solar heat, keeps light; reduces window heat gain **64% vs ordinary glass** (illustrated 90° outside / 75° inside).
  - *Bold on Cold* (heating): ~80% of a window's exposed surface is glass, so warmer room-side glass = warmer room and controllable humidity.
  - *Performs above the Norm*: NFRC-rated numbers (below).
- **"Look for the Label"**: compare brands "apples to apples" using the NFRC Energy Performance Label.
- **"Three ways to save"**: annual energy savings · A/C equipment savings · tax benefits.

Sealant layer · four dielectric layers · three silver layers · three barrier-hardener layers · exterior protection layer. The IG unit adds a **stainless steel spacer** and **high-density argon** fill.

## Key Concepts
- **U-Factor**: heat-flow rate through the window (BTU/hr/ft²/°F, winter conditions 0°F out / 70°F in). Lower = better insulation.
- **SHGC**: fraction of solar radiation entering as heat. Lower = less heat gain. (The brochure's definition says "heat loss" — it means heat *gain*.)
- **Visible Transmittance (VT)**: share of visible light passing through. Higher = brighter.
- **UV / fading transmission**: lower = less fading of carpets and furnishings.
- **Condensation Resistance (CR)**: 1–100 NFRC scale; higher resists interior condensation better.
- **Spectrally selective**: coating that passes visible light but blocks infrared heat and UV.
- **NFRC**: National Fenestration Rating Council — independent rating label (brochure prints the URL as "afrc.org"; correct is nfrc.org).
- **ENERGY STAR**: US-government efficiency program; brochure claims Conservation Windows **exceed ENERGY STAR guidelines in every state**.

## Mental Models
- Think of Conservation Glass as **trading a little light for a lot of heat rejection**: VT 66% vs 81% clear double-pane, but SHGC .27 vs .76.
- Use the temperature table for comfort/condensation conversations; use the SHGC/heat-gain figures for Florida cooling conversations.
- Use NFRC label numbers when comparing to competitors — the brochure's own "apples to apples" rule.

## Anti-patterns
- **Mixing center-of-glass and whole-window numbers** in one comparison.
- **Quoting NFRC 0.29/0.19 for an impact window** — wrong product; those are the Tech 2000 double-hung with standard glazing.
- **Understating UV protection**: the approved figure is **99% of UV stopped** (features chart #30). One brochure paragraph says 95%; use 99%.
- **Promising dollar savings**: the only dollar figure is a national aggregate ("over $12 billion per year" if all US single-pane windows were ENERGY STAR). It is not a per-home estimate.
- **Confusing Cardinal's 20-year IG warranty with the Conservation lifetime IG warranty.**

## Worked Example
Homeowner in St. Petersburg asks, "Will these keep my west-facing room cooler?"
1. Cooling question → *Beats the Heat* frame, SHGC is the metric.
2. Center-of-glass: Conservation Glass SHGC .27 vs ordinary low-e .72 and clear double-pane .76; brochure: 64% less heat gain than ordinary glass.
3. Whole window (Tech 2000 DH label, standard double glazing): SHGC 0.19. If the homeowner is buying impact windows, don't use this number — use that unit's own NFRC label.
4. Side benefit: stops 99% of UV → furnishings fade less.
5. Trade-off to disclose: VT 66% — slightly less daylight than clear glass, but no tint to spoil the view.
6. Don't quote savings in dollars; point to the NFRC label for comparing other quotes.

## Key Takeaways
1. Conservation Glass = dual-pane, argon, triple-silver low-E, stainless spacer.
2. Center-of-glass: U .29/.24, SHGC .27, VT 66%, UV .05; stops 99% of UV; equal to 5 panes; 50% quieter.
3. Tech 2000 DH NFRC label: U 0.29, SHGC 0.19, VT 0.45, AL 0.1, CR 59 — standard double glazing, not impact; never quote as an impact-window spec.
4. 64% less heat gain vs ordinary glass; 52°F room-side glass at −20°F outside.
5. Use NFRC labels for competitor comparisons; never promise per-home savings.
