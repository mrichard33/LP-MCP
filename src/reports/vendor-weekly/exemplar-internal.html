Part A · What you may not claim out loud

The 142-lead gap is derived, not measured. It is only what MyHomePros' 5.77% implies against our 32 sets. It could just as easily be an intake defect on our side. Ask for their delivered count; do not assert the gap. Let the number come out of their mouth.

Never say leads went uncalled. You would be wrong. LP's per-lead call_count under-reports badly. Prospect 456361 (JAX, delivered Sep 2) shows zero calls in LP and 18 dial attempts over 13 days with 11 live contacts in Outbound IQ. Two more "zero-call" samples were reached on day one — dispositioned "Renter — mobile home" and "Not Interested."

Do not allege out-of-market delivery. 28.3% of September leads carry a non-Florida area code, up from 23.5% in August — but only 5 of 413 have a non-Florida property address. That is snowbirds and ported cell numbers, not bad geo. You may ask about it. You may not accuse.

Toni already explained Fort Myers — do not demand an explanation. Her Sep 9 recap email states it plainly: Fort Myers 70–80 bracket lowered to $51 CPL. That is the cause of the 3.0→1.8/day drop, and our daily counts confirm it. The argument is not "why did volume fall," it is "you cut price in the one market earning 9% net cost of marketing." Asking her to explain something she put in writing before the meeting costs you credibility for the rest of the call.

Do not name our other vendors — in writing or out loud. The shared sheet lists them as Paid internet vendor A, B and C by design. The control-group argument works without the names; naming them hands MyHomePros intelligence on rivals in their own market and exposes our source mix. Toni did the same thing on Sep 9 — she cited a competing window buyer at 14% without naming them. Mirror it.

Every cost figure is ours and provisional. LP 136 Mktg Sub-Source Cost Analysis 2 carries no Reece cost data — $0 total cost, $0 cost per lead. The $1,377 per issued lead is derived from August's ~$50/raw-lead actual. Do not defend it as fact; use it to force the real number.

Our own two reports disagree. 136 Mktg Sub-Source Cost Analysis 2 shows 4 sold / 8 demos on a lead-created basis; 137 Sales Efficiency By Mode shows 6 closes / 15 demos on an appointment-date basis; LP Supabase counts 413 raw where 136 Mktg Sub-Source Cost Analysis 2 counts 417. If pressed, show both. Do not pick one.

Part C · Word-for-word speaking points

1 · Open with the number — before anything else

"Toni, before we get into optimization, I want to put one number on the table, because I think last week's meeting ran on the wrong premise.

You opened Sep 9 saying our set rate had declined to 5.77%. I went and measured it in Lead Perfection. For September 1 through 15 we show 413 leads and 32 appointments set. That's 7.75%.

And it's not down — it's up. Same-age August window, August 1 through 15, was 502 leads and 35 sets. That's 6.97%. So the set rate went up almost a full point."

Then stop talking. Let Toni or Annie answer. Do not fill the silence with the 142-lead arithmetic — make them explain their number first.

2 · The reconciliation — only after they respond

"So help me understand where 5.77% comes from. If your numerator is the same 32 sets, then your denominator has to be about 555 leads. We received 413. That's roughly 142 leads' difference.

I'm not saying you didn't deliver them. It could just as easily be something breaking on our intake. But it matters, because in August the gap was 26 leads and now it looks like 142 — and we paused four markets last week based on your number, not mine.

What's your delivered count for September 1 through 15?"

If they produce a number: get it by market and by day, and confirm the Friday deadline in writing anyway. If they don't have it: "Then that's the first deliverable, and it's Friday at five."

3 · Give the credit early — and mean it

"Two things I want to give you credit for before I push on anything else.

One, you executed the pause. I checked it. Jacksonville, Lakeland, Sarasota, Fort Lauderdale — all four went to zero leads on September 10. That was clean and it was on time.

Two, duplicates are not a problem with you. Three repeat phone numbers inside 413 leads, and 21 that were already in the house from another source. That's five percent. That's clean delivery and I'm not going to pretend otherwise."

4 · The concession — say it before you use the control group

"And I owe you one thing, so let me say it out loud.

We tightened our confirmation standards in September to screen out no-homes and one-legs before they issue. That's real and it does move set-to-issued. Though I'll tell you what I found — it cost about 1.7 points. August was 48.6% set-to-issued, September is 46.9%. So it's real, but it isn't the story.

You've got the per-lead disposition file — 135 Lead Disposition Detail 2 — so Annie has what she needs for the Jacksonville back-credits. What I need back against it is the tier mix by market.

Where I'm behind is the automated feed — 135 Lead Disposition Detail 2 and 136 Mktg Sub-Source Cost Analysis 2 landing in your inbox on a schedule instead of me sending them by hand. I said I'd stand that up on the 2nd and I haven't. That's this week."

5 · The control group — this kills the call-center excuse

"I did want to test whether this was us, because that's the obvious explanation and you were right to raise it.

Over the identical days, our contact rate on the dialer went from 12.4% to 23.9%. It nearly doubled. We placed fewer dials — 118,000 down to 92,000 — and got more contacts and more sales out of them.

And across our other paid sources, same days: one fell from 6.6% to 3.6%. Another fell from 54% to 46%. A third was flat. Our canvass was flat. You went up.

So whatever's happening, our floor didn't get worse — and you're the only paid source that improved."

If "are all the leads getting called?" comes back:

"They're getting called. I pulled one at random — prospect 456361, a Jacksonville lead you delivered on the 2nd. Our system shows zero calls on it. The actual dialer records show 18 attempts over 13 days and 11 times someone picked up and hung up. Our per-lead counter is just wrong. I'm not going to bring you a 'nobody called them' argument, because it isn't true."

6 · The real problem — job size, not conversion

The most useful thing you say all call. It hands them a fixable target and moves the argument off set rate.

"Here's what I actually think is wrong, and it's not conversion.

When your leads reach a rep, they perform better than the company average. Net-issue 88% against our 70%. Close rate 40% against our 32%. On-time demo 24% against 17%. Your leads convert.

They just buy small. Average sold job on a MyHomePros lead is $17,816. Company average is $25,166. You're 29% below.

That's exactly what Brad was describing — the $200,000 house. They'll sit, they'll demo, they'll even buy, but they buy a fraction of a job. So the fix isn't set rate. The fix is home value, and that's the zip list. I'm building it off our sold-job addresses and you'll have it Monday.

And I want to add average sold job size to the scorecard, because if we optimize on set rate alone we'll make this worse, not better."

7 — Fort Myers — a disagreement, not a miss. Deliver flatly.

She told you what she did. Credit it first, then argue the direction.

"Your recap was clear and I appreciate getting it same-day. I can see all four changes in our numbers, so I know they went live.

But I want to push on one of them. You lowered the Fort Myers 70-to-80 bracket to $51. Our daily counts show Fort Myers hitting five and seven leads a day before the 10th, and it hasn't broken three on any day since.

Fort Myers is my best market. It sets at 18.4% and it runs 9% net cost of marketing since January. That's the one place where I want to pay more, not less. Cutting price there saves me a few dollars a lead and costs me the leads themselves.

Meanwhile St. Pete got cheaper pricing and went from 5.6 to 7.3 a day, and St. Pete sets at 3.2%. Ninety-four leads, three appointments, zero sales.

So I'd like the Fort Myers cut reversed and the 80-plus bucket bid up there. If cost of marketing is the concern, take it out of St. Pete, not out of Fort Myers."

8 · Lakeland — say it once, then let it sit

"And for the record — Lakeland went live on your recommendation on August 24th. Sixteen days later we paused it having spent about $5,400 for zero sales. Fort Lauderdale, $1,600, also zero. I'm not relitigating it. I just want it on the record before we turn anything else on."

9 · Close with the consequence

"So here's where I am. On September 2nd we both agreed cost per issued lead was the leading indicator. Two weeks later neither of us can calculate it, because I don't have your actual spend and we don't agree on the delivered count.

My provisional math puts us at about $1,377 per issued lead against a $500 target. I don't want to hold you to a number I derived myself, so send me the real one.

Actual spend and delivered count for September 1 through 15, by market, Friday at five. If it's not in by then, I'm capping spend at the current run rate and freezing all optimization — no new markets, no bid changes, no tier changes — until we agree on one delivered number.

Next review is Wednesday the 23rd. Two metrics carry it: cost per issued lead under $500, and set rate at 10% or better on whatever delivered number we agree on."
